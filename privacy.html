This policy covers Unrepost: Repost Remover for iPhone and for Android.

The short version

Unrepost has no server and no account system. There is nothing to sign up for with us. Your repost list, your removal history and your settings are created on your device and stay there. We cannot see them, because they are never sent anywhere.

To find and fix problems, the app sends crash reports and anonymous usage statistics to Google’s Firebase. They never include your TikTok handle, which videos you reposted or which ones you removed, and they are not linked to your name, email or TikTok account. See Crash reports and usage statistics.

We do not sell or share personal data, we do not use advertising software, and we do not track you across other apps or websites. On Android, the app does not collect the advertising ID, and the permissions that would allow it are removed from the app at build time.

How signing in to TikTok works

To list your reposts, the app needs to be signed in as you. It does that by showing you TikTok’s own sign-in page inside an embedded browser window. You type your credentials into TikTok, over an encrypted connection, on a page we do not read.

We never see, receive or store your TikTok password. What results is a standard session cookie, held only in the embedded browser’s own storage on your device (on iPhone, the app’s local website storage; on Android, the app’s WebView storage) — the same way a browser holds one. It is never copied to us or to anyone else. On Android it is also excluded from the automatic backup, so it is not copied to Google Drive and does not travel to a new phone: you sign in again there. Signing out from the account screen erases it, and so does deleting the app.

The app reads only what your own profile already shows: the reposts on it, their creators, captions and thumbnails. It does not post, follow, message, or read anything private.

Purchases

Subscriptions are sold by the store you installed the app from — Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android — and processed through your account with that store. We never see your payment details.

We use RevenueCat to check whether a subscription is active. RevenueCat receives purchase transaction information and a randomly generated identifier for your installation. That identifier is not your name, your email or your TikTok handle, and it is not linked to the contents of your profile. The store’s handling of your purchase is covered by its own privacy policy: Apple’s or Google’s. On Android, you can view or cancel a subscription at any time in the Google Play app, under Payments and subscriptions.

Crash reports and usage statistics

The app uses two parts of Google’s Firebase:

  • Crashlytics. If the app crashes, it sends a crash report: where in the app’s code the crash happened, the app and operating system (iOS or Android) versions, and the device model. We use these to fix the crash.
  • Analytics. The app sends anonymous usage statistics, such as which screens are opened, how long the app is used for, whether signing in, a scan and a removal run completed, how many reposts a scan found or a run removed (as a range, such as “10–49”, never the exact number), when a subscription is bought (the plan and its price, never your payment details), the settings of a clean-up reminder when you save one (whether it covers reposts or likes, daily or weekly, and the hour), the app and operating system versions, the device model, and an approximate region that Google works out from the internet connection. We use these to understand which parts of the app work and which need improving.

Both are tied only to a random identifier that Firebase creates for this installation of the app. It is not your name, your email, your TikTok handle or the advertising identifier — the app does not collect the advertising identifier at all — and deleting the app discards it. Neither includes your TikTok session, your repost list, your removal history or anything else you see in the app. We do not use this information for advertising, and it is not used to track you. Google processes it on our behalf under Google’s privacy policy.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the app uses them for two things, both created on your device: to tell you how a removal run is going or how it ended while the app is in the background, and to remind you of a clean-up you scheduled. There are no marketing notifications, and nothing is pushed to the app from a server — we do not have one.

Removing your data

  • Sign out from the account screen to erase the TikTok session.
  • Erase the removal history at any time in Settings. You can export it as a CSV first if you want to keep a copy.
  • Delete (uninstall) the app to remove everything it stored on your device. On Android, if you have backup switched on, also remove the app’s backup in the Google One app under Device backups.

We hold no account or profile data about you. Crash reports and usage statistics are tied only to the random installation identifier described above, and deleting the app starts a new one. If you would like a subscription record, crash reports or usage statistics removed, contact us and we will pass the request to RevenueCat or Google.

Removals are permanent

Removing a repost is carried out on your TikTok account at your instruction, and TikTok provides no way to undo it. The app keeps a local record of what was removed, including the video link, so that you have one. That record is only on your device.

Children

Unrepost is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
